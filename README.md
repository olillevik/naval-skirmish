# Naval Skirmish

A top-down browser game about steering a small dinghy across a round magical sea that ends in open space. The latest build of `main` is live at <https://olillevik.github.io/naval-skirmish/>.

Right now you can sail the dinghy around the Arena with the keyboard or, on a phone, by touch. Tap or click the start screen to begin a Run. On a phone, the tap also asks the browser for fullscreen, and the game asks you to turn the phone to landscape. W or ↑ raises the throttle, S or ↓ lowers it, and A/D or ←/→ turn. The throttle stays where you leave it. A ring on the water marks where the Rim current starts pulling the dinghy toward the Edge. A warning shows once the pull is too strong to row against, and a dinghy that crosses the Edge falls into space and ends the Run. On a touch screen, a throttle lever on the right edge sets the throttle directly and shows its value. Dragging sideways anywhere on the left half steers, harder the further you drag, and the dinghy goes straight again when you lift your thumb. Both thumbs work at once.

A Run starts with a 5-second countdown, and then Wave 1 arrives. Wave n has 2 + n enemy dinghies, drawn over a red ring. They sail at your dinghy and circle it just inside Arrow range, and they turn back before the Rim current. From Wave 4, Wave n also has (n − 2) ÷ 2 enemy ships, rounded down, in black, red, green, blue or yellow. A ship has 120 health, a top speed of 200 px/s against a dinghy's 150, and turns and gets going more slowly. Like your dinghy, a ship shows fire when it is badly damaged. Enemy health grows 5% each Wave. Every Crew fires a Volley once a second, 1 Arrow from a dinghy and 3 slightly spread Arrows from a ship, at the closest vessel on the other side within 350 px. An Arrow flies straight to where its target was, so a moving vessel can dodge it. A vessel with no health left sinks and fades away. An enemy you push over the Edge is lost too, the same as one you sink. When your dinghy and an enemy start touching, both take damage in proportion to how fast they close, 10 each for two dinghies meeting head-on at full speed. Enemies that bump into each other only push apart. Your dinghy has 100 health, shows fire when it is badly damaged, and starts to regenerate 3 s after the last hit. Its health carries over from one Wave to the next. When a Wave has no enemies left, the next countdown starts. Each enemy you lose, sunk or over the Edge, credits you 5 Gold for a dinghy or 20 for a ship. The Wave number, the countdown, your health and your Gold show at the top left, and each enemy has a health bar. Press 1 or Space, or tap the Fireball button at the foot of the throttle lever on a phone, and the Captain throws a Fireball at the enemy within 600 px that the Targeting rule picks, the closest one unless you set another rule in the Cabin. It curves after its target at up to 90° a second, explodes for 40 damage on its first hit, and burns out after 3 s if it misses. The cooldown is 6 s, and a press with no enemy in range does nothing and costs nothing. The HUD shows the cooldown, and the phone button shows the cooldown or "No target". From Wave 3, each enemy has a 5% chance of being a Wizard vessel, with at most one in a Wave. A Wizard vessel has a pulsing purple ring under its hull and a purple flag beside its health bar. Its Wizard throws a Fireball at your dinghy whenever it is within 600 px and the Wizard's 10 s cooldown is ready, by the same rules as the Captain's but for 25 damage. Losing a Wizard vessel credits 3× its class's Gold. The Run ends when your dinghy sinks or falls off the Edge, and enemies lost after that credit nothing. The game-over screen says which way the Run ended, and shows your Score (the Waves you defeated) and the Best score on this device, which the browser keeps in local storage.

Press E or Esc, or tap the Cabin button in the top right corner on a phone, to open the Cabin. It pauses the game completely, Wave countdown included, and E, Esc or its close button resume it exactly where it stopped. The Cabin opens only while your dinghy is afloat, and the HUD shows a mark while it can. It shows your Gold, health and vessel, and sells Repairs: 10 Gold each restores 25% of max health, up to max, as often as you can pay. Repair is disabled at full health. It also sells seven Upgrades, each taking effect at once. Max health adds 25% of the dinghy's base max health, to current health too, for 30, 60 and 120 Gold. Regen adds 1% of max health per second for 25, 50 and 100 Gold. Arrow rate cuts the time between Volleys from 1 s to 0.8, 0.65 and 0.5 s for 20, 40 and 80 Gold. Volley size adds an Arrow, up to 5, for 30, 60, 120 and 240 Gold. Flaming arrows, for 80 Gold, set every enemy your Crew's Arrows hit burning for 3 s at 2 damage per second. A new hit restarts the time, burns don't stack, and a burning enemy shows fire. An enemy the burn sinks credits Gold like any other. Fireball damage raises the Captain's Fireball from 40 damage to 55, 70 and 90, and Fireball cooldown cuts the Captain's cooldown from 6 s to 5, 4 and 3 s, each for 30, 60 and 120 Gold. A cooldown already running keeps its time left, and neither changes a Wizard vessel's Fireballs. The Cabin also sells the "farthest", "lowest health" and "highest health" Targeting rules for 25 Gold each, and its Targeting rule picker lists the rules you own and marks the active one. A Run starts owning only "closest". A rule picks only among enemies within Fireball range, ties go to the closest, and it stays active until you pick another. A Fireball whose target is lost before it lands picks a new target within 600 px of itself by the active rule. Each row shows its level, what the next level gives and its price, or "maxed" ("owned" for a Targeting rule). Purchases reset with each new Run.

## Run it

You need Node.js 22.12 or newer (CI uses Node.js 24). Install the dependencies once with `npm install`, then run `npm run dev` and open the URL Vite prints (it ends in `/naval-skirmish/`).

## Test it

`npm test` runs the Vitest unit tests against the game core, with no browser. `npm run test:e2e` builds the game and runs the Playwright smoke tests against that build. Before the first browser run, install Chromium with `npx playwright install chromium`. `npm run typecheck` runs the TypeScript compiler.

The browser tests read the game state through a read-only test hook, `window.navalSkirmishTest.state`. The hook exists only when the page URL has the `?test` flag. Each Playwright project runs the spec file with its name: `desktop`, `phone-landscape` and `phone-upright`.

## How the code is laid out

All game rules live in `src/core`, which is plain TypeScript that never imports Phaser (see [ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-pure-game-core-with-phaser-view.md)). The core has four operations: `createWorld(seed, config)`, `step(world, commands)`, `readState(world)` and `applyCabinAction(world, action)`, which applies a Cabin purchase or Targeting rule choice with no time passing. The Phaser view in `src/view` draws the core's state and calls `step` once per fixed tick of 1/60 s. Domain words such as dinghy, Arena and Edge are defined in [CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md). The view's UI text is in `src/view/text.ts`.

The vessels are 3D models from the Pirate Kit, kept in `art/pirate-kit`. `npm run render-sprites` draws each model from 64 headings into one sprite sheet in `public/assets/kit`, and writes the frame sizes to `src/view/kitSheets.json`. It also draws the water tile, `public/assets/kit/water.png`, in the same flat style. The view shows the frame that matches a vessel's heading, instead of turning one image. The camera tilt, the light, each sheet's size and sail colour, and the water's colours and waves are constants in `scripts/render-sprites`. Change one, run the script again and commit what it writes.

GitHub Actions runs the type check and both test suites on every push and pull request. When they pass on `main`, it deploys the build to GitHub Pages.
